Abstract
This paper summarizes the observed efficiency regression in Hephaestus from the 40-60 range down to approximately 25 on June 2-3, 2026. The analysis uses repository telemetry artifacts and run outputs to identify proximate causes, root causes, and practical recovery actions. The central finding is that the dominant score loss came from a throughput-window collapse (completed work in the last 24 hours dropping from 55 to 0), with secondary erosion from retry growth.

Primary Quantitative Findings
1. Throughput collapse explains most of the drop.
   - High band snapshot (2026-05-29 13:09): completedLast24h=55, throughputScore=1
   - Low band snapshot (2026-06-02 15:46): completedLast24h=0, throughputScore=0
   - Latency and completion components remained near-constant across the drop.

2. Retry growth explains the additional decline from 25.867 to 24.696.
   - 2026-06-02 15:46: retryRate=0.082, retryPenaltyScore=0.918
   - 2026-06-03 14:03: retryRate=0.199, retryPenaltyScore=0.801

3. Latency was not the dominant cause of this specific regression.
   - p95 admission->complete remained effectively unchanged in the low-band period.

Operational Root Cause Signals
From upgrade telemetry and Stage A findings:
1. No completions in last 24h (explicit alert).
2. Retry pressure increased while completion count remained flat.
3. Persistent high-failure buckets include:
   - unsupported task envelope
   - command is not allowlisted
   - file scope/plan boundary violations
   - stale recovery after interrupted attempts
4. Stage A baseline had NO-GO gate result due to:
   - low efficiency score
   - high blocked ticket count
   - backend reliability below threshold

Interpretation
The system did not lose efficiency because of a sudden latency explosion. It lost efficiency because completed work exited the 24-hour window and was not replenished. Once throughputScore fell from 1 to 0, the overall index dropped by roughly 35 points. After that, rising retry pressure reduced the score further.

What Restores The 40-60 Band
Near-term target conditions (inferred from historical snapshots):
1. completedLast24h >= 9 tends to re-enter ~40 band.
2. completedLast24h >= 11 tends to re-enter ~50 band.
3. retryRate <= 0.10 stabilizes upward movement.
4. maintain single-daemon continuity to reduce stale-recovery churn.

Recommended Recovery Sequence
1. Stabilize execution:
   - ensure exactly one daemon process remains running.
   - avoid restart churn during active waves.
2. Prioritize high-yield ticket cohorts:
   - run low-risk FIX/UPDATE/BUILD style tickets first.
3. Quarantine high-failure cohorts temporarily:
   - unsupported envelope and repeated file-boundary violators.
4. Reduce retry inflation:
   - pause auto-retry for chronic offenders until constraints are corrected.
5. Improve backend reliability:
   - treat model/backend reliability as an efficiency prerequisite.
6. Measure every 60-90 minutes:
   - metrics:efficiency and tickets metrics
   - verify completedLast24h rises and retryRate trends down.

Risk Notes
1. Lean gating can allow operations to continue while strict performance gates remain unmet.
2. If throughput remains at 0, score cannot recover meaningfully regardless of minor quality improvements.
3. If retries continue rising above ~0.15, score recovery will stall even if some throughput returns.

Conclusion
The June 3 efficiency state (~24.7) is primarily a throughput-window starvation condition with secondary retry drag. The fastest path back to 40-60 is to restore consistent 24-hour completions and suppress retry-heavy failure classes. This is operationally achievable without major architectural change, provided queue hygiene and daemon continuity are enforced.
