# Amodei2016

Citation: Amodei, D., Olah, C., Steinhardt, J., Christiano, P., Schulman, J., and Mané, D. (2016). Concrete Problems in AI Safety.

Public URL or DOI: http://arxiv.org/abs/1606.06565v2

Status: copy+note
Blueprint Phases: D3-D5

## Summary

- Breaks AI safety into concrete engineering failure modes instead of abstract ethics language.
- Highlights reward hacking, unsafe exploration, distribution shift, and scalable oversight problems.
- Encourages design-time safeguards rather than post hoc trust in good intentions.
- Useful because it turns vague risk talk into named classes of failure.

## Relevance To Hephaestus

- D3-D5 need explicit safeguards against unsafe autonomy, brittle optimization, and hidden failure accumulation.
- Approval boundaries and promotion rules should be framed as responses to concrete failure classes.
- Safety evidence should be attached to execution policy, not left to operator intuition.

## Hephaestus Would Be Worse Without It

Hephaestus could discuss safety in slogans while missing the concrete failure patterns that matter operationally.
