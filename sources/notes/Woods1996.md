## Summary

- Automation often looks simpler from a distance than it is in actual operation.
- Complexity does not disappear when a task is automated; it moves into new coordination and failure modes.
- Operators need support for supervising automation, not just toggling it on and off.
- High-level autopilot abstractions can hide fragile inner dependencies.

## Relevance To Hephaestus

- Autopilot should stay bounded and transparent rather than becoming a vague central intelligence.
- Control-plane workflows should expose dependencies, not compress them into a false single state.
- D3-D6 need this as a guardrail against overly magical task orchestration.

## Hephaestus Would Be Worse Without It

Hephaestus could centralize automation power in ways that look elegant but create brittle hidden coupling and opaque failure modes.
